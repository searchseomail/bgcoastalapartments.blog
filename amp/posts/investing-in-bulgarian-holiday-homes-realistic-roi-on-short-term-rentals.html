Investing in Bulgarian Holiday Homes: Realistic ROI on Short-Term Rentals

NeonGlitch
31.08.2026

Bulgaria has quietly transformed from an undiscovered Eastern European secret into one of the most compelling real estate markets for lifestyle and investment buyers alike. With affordable entry prices, a stunning Black Sea coastline, dramatic mountain ranges, and a low cost of living, the Balkan nation attracts millions of tourists annually. For international investors, the allure of purchasing a holiday home goes beyond personal use—it represents a tangible asset capable of generating substantial passive income through short-term vacation rentals like Airbnb and Booking.com.

However, navigating the landscape of Bulgarian holiday homes requires more than just falling in view of a sandy beach or a snow-capped ski slope. To achieve a realistic Return on Investment (ROI), investors must look past glossy marketing brochures and dive deep into actual occupancy rates, operational overheads, seasonal fluctuations, and legal frameworks. This comprehensive guide breaks down everything you need to know about calculating and maximizing your ROI on short-term rentals in Bulgaria.

The Bulgarian Real Estate Landscape: Where to Invest

Before calculating returns, you must choose the right location. Bulgaria’s property market is generally divided into three distinct segments for short-term rentals: coastal resorts, historical towns, and ski destinations.

The Black Sea Coast

The coastline is the heavyweight champion of seasonal tourism in Bulgaria. Major hubs like Sunny Beach and Golden Sands offer high volumes of tourists, driven by nightlife, budget-friendly amenities, and expansive beaches. However, these areas are fiercely competitive and intensely seasonal, typically running at full capacity for only 60 to 90 days a year. On the other hand, quieter coastal spots offer a different demographic. For instance, many buyers find long-term stability and high-end appeal by exploring real estate in Sozopol, where ancient town charm attracts affluent cultural tourists willing to pay premium nightly rates.

Ski Resorts

Bansko, Borovets, and Pamporovo cater to winter sports enthusiasts. Bansko, in particular, has evolved into a year-round digital nomad hub, which helps mitigate the traditional "off-season" drop-off seen in pure holiday locations. Winter yields high nightly rates, though spring and autumn can see a significant lull unless marketed aggressively toward remote workers and hikers.

Realistic ROI Expectations: The Numbers Game

When real estate agents throw around phrases like "guaranteed 10% ROI," it is vital to separate gross rental yields from net cash-on-cash returns. Let's look at the financial mechanics of a typical one-bedroom apartment in a prime Bulgarian coastal or ski location.

Expense / Metric Percentage / Estimated Cost
Purchase Price (1-Bed Apartment) €60,000 - €80,000
Gross Rental Yield 8% - 12%
Maintenance & Management Fees 15% - 25% of gross revenue
Platform Commissions (Airbnb/Booking) 3% - 15%
Utilities, Internet & Insurance €1,000 - €1,500 annually
Realistic Net ROI 4% - 7% annually

While a net ROI of 4% to 7% might sound modest compared to high-risk stock portfolios, it is competitive within European real estate standards. Furthermore, this does not account for potential capital appreciation over time as Bulgaria edges closer to Eurozone integration and Schengen adoption.

Key Factors Impacting Your Short-Term Rental ROI

Achieving the upper tier of those return figures is not accidental. It requires meticulous planning and a strong grasp of operational factors.

  • Seasonality: Unlike urban centers like Sofia, holiday homes face sharp peaks and valleys. Maximizing off-season occupancy through creative marketing (such as targeting remote workers or wellness retreats) is critical.
  • Property Management: If you do not live in Bulgaria, you will need a reliable property management company. They typically charge between 20% and 30% of rental income to handle guest communication, check-ins, cleaning, and laundry. While this cuts into profits, it protects your asset and maintains Superhost ratings.
  • Complex Maintenance Fees (Taksa Podderzhka): Most Bulgarian holiday apartments are located within gated complexes featuring pools, security, and landscaped gardens. Annual maintenance fees range from €6 to €15 per square meter. Always factor this fixed cost into your yield calculations.

Legal and Tax Considerations for Foreign Investors

Bulgaria is exceptionally welcoming to foreign property buyers. Non-EU citizens can freely buy buildings and apartments (though owning the underlying land requires setting up a local company, which is a straightforward and inexpensive process).

However, buying property requires absolute diligence. Before handing over any deposits, ensure you thoroughly investigate the legal status of the building. It is equally important to know how to check a property in Bulgaria for debts, liens, and encumbrances to avoid inheriting previous owners' financial liabilities or facing developer bankruptcies.

Once you reach the final transaction stage, pay extreme attention to the legal drafting of the transfer document. Ensuring your legal counsel reviews all stipulations in the official agreement is vital, much like understanding every clause in the Notary Act in Bulgaria before signing it in front of a state notary.

On the tax front, Bulgaria boasts one of the most favorable tax regimes in the European Union:

  • Corporate Tax: 10% (if operating through a registered company).
  • Personal Income Tax on Rental Income: A flat 10%, with a standard 10% statutory deduction for expenses, resulting in an effective tax rate of just 9%.
  • Local Property Taxes: Generally very low, ranging between 0.05% and 0.45% of the property's tax valuation (which is usually significantly lower than market value).

Step-by-Step Strategy to Maximize Your Holiday Home ROI

If you want to turn a standard vacation property into a high-performing short-term rental machine, implement these proven strategies:

1. Invest in Interior Design and Photography

Guests browsing Airbnb or Booking.com make split-second decisions based on visuals. Properties with modern, minimalist, Scandinavian-style furniture, bright lighting, and professional photographs command nightly rates 20-30% higher than outdated, cookie-cutter apartments.

2. Automate Guest Access

Install smart lock systems. Delayed flights and late-night arrivals are common in tourist hubs. Smart locks allow seamless self-check-in, drastically reducing management headaches and labor costs.

3. Diversify Booking Channels

Do not rely solely on Airbnb. List your property across multiple channels including Booking.com, Vrbo, and local Bulgarian travel platforms, while also building a direct-booking social media presence to bypass platform commissions entirely.

Conclusion: Is a Bulgarian Holiday Home Worth It?

Investing in Bulgarian holiday homes for short-term rental yields can be a highly rewarding venture, provided you approach it with realistic expectations. While you are unlikely to get rich overnight, a well-selected, professionally managed apartment in a prime coastal or mountain location can comfortably yield a net ROI of 5% to 7%, while also serving as a private, cost-free holiday getaway for you and your family.

By pairing thorough legal due diligence with smart operational management, your Bulgarian property can become a resilient, cash-flowing asset in a growing European market.
