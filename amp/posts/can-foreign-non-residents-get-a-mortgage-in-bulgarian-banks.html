Can Foreign Non-Residents Get a Mortgage in Bulgarian Banks?

ZenithZero
03.10.2026

Bulgaria has long attracted international buyers thanks to its affordable cost of living, picturesque landscapes, vibrant coastal resorts, and expanding urban centers. Whether you are eyeing a seaside apartment, a peaceful retreat near thermal springs as detailed in this overview of Real Estate in Bulgarian Spa Towns: Thermal Springs & Property, or an upscale property, the question of financing is paramount. A common query among prospective international investors is: Can foreign non-residents get a mortgage in Bulgarian banks?

The short answer is yes, it is entirely possible, but it comes with specific stipulations, higher down payment requirements, and stricter documentation processes compared to resident borrowers. Bulgarian commercial banks are open to financing foreign buyers, but they evaluate these applications through a more conservative lens due to the cross-border risk.

Understanding the Legal Landscape for Non-Resident Mortgages

Before diving into bank requirements, it is essential to understand how foreign ownership works in Bulgaria. EU citizens and entities enjoy the same rights as Bulgarian nationals when purchasing buildings and land. Non-EU citizens, however, face certain restrictions regarding land ownership (though they can freely own apartments, houses without land, or buy land if they establish a local Bulgarian company). Bulgarian banks are well-versed in these legal nuances and will verify the property's legal status before approving any financing.

Non-residents generally fall into two categories:

  • EU/EEA Citizens: Often benefit from slightly smoother processing times and terms that closely mirror those of residents.
  • Non-EU Citizens: Face more rigorous vetting, particularly regarding the source of funds, tax compliance, and proof of international income.

Key Requirements and Eligibility Criteria

Securing a mortgage as a non-resident requires meeting several strict criteria set by the Bulgarian National Bank and individual commercial lenders. Because banks cannot easily repossess or audit assets located abroad, they mitigate their risk by demanding robust financial guarantees.

1. Proof of Stable Income

Lenders need absolute assurance that the borrower can service the debt. If your income is generated outside of Bulgaria, you will need to translate and legalize comprehensive financial documents:

  • Employment contracts and recent payslips (usually the last 6 to 12 months).
  • Tax returns from your home country stamped by the relevant tax authority.
  • Bank statements showing regular salary deposits or stable business revenue.

2. Debt-to-Income (DTI) Ratio

Bulgarian banks typically enforce a conservative DTI ratio. Your total monthly debt obligations (including the new mortgage) generally cannot exceed 40% to 50% of your net monthly income.

3. Loan-to-Value (LTV) Ratios for Non-Residents

While Bulgarian residents might secure mortgages with a 15% to 20% down payment, non-residents face much stricter LTV limits. Typically, foreign buyers must finance a larger portion of the purchase price out of pocket:

Borrower Type Maximum LTV Ratio Minimum Down Payment
Bulgarian Resident Up to 80% - 85% 15% - 20%
EU Non-Resident Up to 50% - 60% 40% - 50%
Non-EU Non-Resident Up to 40% - 50% 50% - 60%

Available Loan Terms and Interest Rates

Mortgage terms for non-residents are often shorter than those offered to locals. While residents might stretch a loan over 30 years, non-residents are usually capped at a maximum term of 10 to 15 years, depending on their age at the time of maturity. Most banks require the loan to be fully paid off before the borrower reaches 65 to 70 years of age.

Interest rates in Bulgaria have historically remained competitive compared to the rest of the Eurozone, influenced by the country’s currency board arrangement tied to the Euro. Both fixed and variable interest rate products are available, though variable rates linked to indices like EURIBOR are more common.

Step-by-Step Application Process

Navigating the Bulgarian mortgage application process as a foreigner involves several sequential steps:

  • Property Selection and Valuation: Find your property and sign a preliminary contract. The bank will send an independent licensed appraiser to determine the market value of the real estate.
  • Documentation Gathering: Collect all personal, financial, and property documents. Ensure they are officially translated into Bulgarian by a certified translator and, where applicable, authenticated with an Apostille.
  • Bank Submission: Submit the application to one or more Bulgarian banks. Comparing multiple offers can save you thousands over the life of the loan.
  • Credit Committee Approval: The bank’s risk department evaluates your profile. This can take anywhere from two weeks to a month.
  • Notarization and Mortgage Deed: Upon approval, a Bulgarian notary public formalizes the purchase and registers the bank's mortgage lien on the property.

Alternative Strategies and Long-Term Residency

For those looking beyond seasonal holiday homes, purchasing real estate can serve as a stepping stone toward long-term residency. Many international buyers explore pathways detailed in guides such as the Bulgarian Golden D-Visa and Residency for Retirees: Complete Property Owner Guide, which outlines how property ownership interacts with immigration policies.

Keep in mind that obtaining a mortgage does not automatically grant residency rights, but showing deep economic ties to the country through property ownership and local bank accounts can support various visa applications.

Challenges and Tips for Foreign Mortgage Applicants

While the process is structured, foreign applicants frequently encounter specific hurdles. Being prepared can drastically improve your approval odds:

  • Language Barrier: Few bank officers speak fluent English outside of major cities like Sofia, Plovdiv, or Varna. Hiring a local independent mortgage broker or bilingual legal counsel is highly recommended.
  • Currency Risk: Although the Bulgarian Lev (BGN) is pegged to the Euro at a fixed rate, fluctuations in your home currency against the Euro can impact your ability to service the debt if your income is earned in USD, GBP, or another currency.
  • Hidden Fees: Factor in additional costs such as property valuation fees, bank application fees, notary fees, local taxes, and legal representation, which can add 5% to 10% to the initial purchase expenses.

Conclusion

Getting a mortgage in Bulgaria as a foreign non-resident is entirely achievable, provided you have strong financial credentials, patience, and a substantial down payment. By understanding the conservative LTV limits, preparing legally certified documentation, and working with knowledgeable local professionals, international buyers can successfully finance their dream property on the Black Sea coast, in the mountains, or within Bulgaria's thriving cultural hubs.
