How Foreign Owners Can Sell Property in Bulgaria and Legally Transfer Money Back Home

SolarFlareX
02.10.2026

Selling real estate in Bulgaria as a foreign citizen can be a rewarding culmination of an investment journey, whether you initially purchased a holiday home on the Black Sea coast, a ski chalet in Bansko, or an urban apartment in Sofia. However, transitioning from a property owner to a successfully cashed-out seller involves navigating a specific set of legal, tax, and banking regulations. For many international investors, the primary concern is not just finding a buyer and closing the deal, but ensuring that the proceeds can be legally, efficiently, and safely repatriated back to their home country.

Bulgaria operates under a clear legal framework for foreign property ownership, but compliance with local tax authorities, notaries, and anti-money laundering (AML) banking protocols is mandatory. This comprehensive guide outlines the step-by-step process of selling Bulgarian real estate, managing local tax obligations, and navigating the international money transfer landscape to bring your funds home legally.

1. Preparation and Listing Your Bulgarian Property

Before any money can change hands, you must prepare your property for the market. While some owners choose to manage the sale independently, many foreign owners rely on local agencies, especially if they do not reside full-time in the country. If you previously generated income from your asset, you may already be familiar with local operational standards, perhaps even having navigated Renting Out Your Apartment via Property Management Companies in Bulgaria: Fees and Reviews to handle tenants and maintenance while you were away.

When listing your property, pricing strategy is crucial. Understanding local market liquidity and valuation trends will prevent your property from sitting stagnant on the market. Realistic pricing is often informed by past negotiation trends, similar to how buyers evaluate How Much Can You Negotiate on Bulgarian Property Asking Prices? when making offers. Ensure all your property deeds (Notarial Deed), architectural blueprints, and tax valuations are up to date and physically accessible.

2. Required Documents for the Sale

Bulgarian law requires a strict set of documents to execute a property transfer. Missing paperwork can delay the notary appointment by weeks. As a foreign seller, you must gather:

  • Original Notarial Deed (Нотариален акт): Proves your legal ownership of the property.
  • Tax Valuation Certificate (Данъчна оценка): Issued by the local municipality (Obshtina), this certificate establishes the baseline state value of the property for tax and fee calculations.
  • Sketch of the Property (Скица): Issued by the Cadastre Agency, detailing boundaries and technical characteristics.
  • Certificate of No Encumbrances (Удостоверение за тежести): Proves the property is free of mortgages, injunctions, or third-party rights.
  • Identity Documents: Valid international passports for all registered owners.
  • Marriage Certificate (if applicable): If the property was acquired during marriage, both spouses must sign, or a proper power of attorney must be presented.
  • Energy Performance Certificate (EPC): A mandatory document rating the energy efficiency of the building.

3. Taxes and Fees Associated with the Sale

Failing to account for taxes and fees can eat into your net proceeds. In Bulgaria, transaction costs are typically shared between the buyer and seller, though specific terms can be negotiated in the preliminary contract.

Capital Gains Tax (CGT)

The standard corporate and personal income tax rate in Bulgaria is a flat 10%. However, under the Bulgarian Personal Income Tax Act, you may be exempt from paying capital gains tax if:

  • You have owned the property for more than 3 years (calculated from the date of acquisition to the date of sale).
  • You are selling only one residential property within the calendar year, regardless of how long you owned it.
  • You are selling up to two real estates (residential, agricultural, or forest land) provided more than 5 years have passed since the acquisition of the first property.

If you do not meet these exemption criteria, you must declare the profit (selling price minus the documented purchase price and allowable improvement expenses) and pay a 10% tax.

Local Municipal Taxes and Notary Fees

Local transfer taxes usually range from 0.1% to 3% of the property value (depending on the municipality) and are traditionally split 50/50 between buyer and seller. Notary fees are calculated on a sliding scale based on the property's value.

4. The Role of the Bulgarian Notary and Closing the Deal

In Bulgaria, property ownership transfers officially only when a registered Bulgarian Notary signs the final Title Deed. Neither private contracts nor handshake agreements hold legal weight for transfer of ownership.

If you cannot travel to Bulgaria for the closing, you can appoint a local lawyer or trusted representative via a Power of Attorney (POA). If signed abroad, the POA must be notarized and bear an Apostille (for Hague Convention countries) or be properly legalized by the Bulgarian embassy.

Payment is traditionally executed via bank transfer directly to the seller's Bulgarian bank account or via a secure escrow account established by the notary or a commercial bank.

5. Legally Transferring Money Back Home (Repatriation)

Once the sale is finalized and funds are safely sitting in your bank account in Bulgaria, the next step is transferring the money back to your home country. Because Bulgaria is part of the European Union and uses a currency pegged to the Euro (BGN to EUR fixed rate), moving funds within the EU is relatively seamless. However, non-EU residents must clear standard international anti-money laundering (AML) hurdles.

Step-by-Step Guide to Repatriating Funds:

  • Maintain a Paper Trail: Bulgarian commercial banks are legally bound by strict EU anti-money laundering regulations. To transfer a large lump sum abroad, your bank will demand proof of the origin of funds. Keep your original Notarial Deed of purchase, the new Notarial Deed of sale, bank statements showing the incoming buyer funds, and tax clearance certificates.
  • Choose Your Transfer Method: You can use traditional SWIFT bank transfers, SEPA transfers (if transferring to an EU/EEA bank account in Euros), or specialized foreign exchange (FX) brokers. FX brokers often offer significantly better exchange rates and lower transfer fees than traditional high-street banks for large cross-border transactions.
  • Declare the Funds (if required): Depending on your destination country's tax and financial intelligence laws (e.g., the IRS in the US, HMRC in the UK, or equivalent tax agencies), you may need to report the incoming foreign capital. While Bulgaria does not levy an exit tax on capital transfer, your home country may tax worldwide income if you are a tax resident there. Always consult a cross-border tax specialist.

6. Summary Checklist for Foreign Sellers

To ensure a frictionless transaction, keep these golden rules in mind:

  • Verify that your municipal property taxes and utility bills (electricity, water, heating) are fully paid off. Municipalities will block transactions if there are outstanding municipal dues.
  • Hire an independent English-speaking lawyer to represent your interests, ensuring you do not fall victim to common pitfalls that mirror Top 10 Mistakes Foreigners Make When Buying Real Estate in Bulgaria—many of which apply equally to the selling side, such as neglecting clear title representation.
  • Notify your home country's tax authorities if required, ensuring complete transparency and compliance with international tax reporting standards (such as CRS - Common Reporting Standard).

By approaching the sale methodically, maintaining meticulous documentation for compliance officers, and utilizing professional legal and financial assistance, foreign owners can successfully liquidate their Bulgarian real estate assets and transfer their capital back home safely and legally.
