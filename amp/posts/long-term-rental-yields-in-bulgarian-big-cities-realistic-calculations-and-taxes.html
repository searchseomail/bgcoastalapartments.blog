Long-Term Rental Yields in Bulgarian Big Cities: Realistic Calculations and Taxes

ByteSized
26.08.2026

Bulgaria’s real estate market has long captured the attention of foreign investors and local buyers alike, largely due to its relatively low entry barriers and the historical perception of high property appreciation. However, while holiday homes and coastal retreats often steal the spotlight, the true backbone of a sustainable real estate investment portfolio lies in urban long-term rentals. Major economic hubs like Sofia, Plovdiv, Varna, and Burgas offer a starkly different investment landscape compared to seasonal resort towns. Understanding the nuances of long-term rental yields in these metropolitan centers requires moving past speculative hype and diving into hard numbers, realistic calculation methodologies, and local taxation frameworks.

Whether you are comparing inland economic powerhouses or buying property in Varna vs Burgas, urban centers present a year-round tenant pool driven by IT professionals, corporate expats, university students, and local families. To evaluate whether these markets align with your financial goals, let us break down the mathematics of yields, the hidden costs of property management, and the strict legalities of Bulgarian taxation.

Deconstructing Long-Term Rental Yields: Gross vs. Net

A common pitfall for novice investors is relying entirely on gross rental yields. Gross yield offers a quick baseline figure, but it fails to account for the ongoing expenses that erode your bottom line. To calculate your true financial return, you must master both formulas.

The Formulas

    Gross Rental Yield (%) = (Annual Rental Income / Purchase Price of Property) × 100

    Net Rental Yield (%) = [(Annual Rental Income – Annual Operating Expenses & Taxes) / (Purchase Price + Acquisition Costs)] × 100

In Bulgaria's big cities, gross yields historically hover between 4.5% and 7.5%, depending on the micro-location, property condition, and size. However, once you factor in notary fees, local taxes, maintenance funds, insurance, and vacancies, your net yield typically drops by 1.5% to 2.5% points. For example, a two-bedroom apartment purchased in Sofia for €120,000 generating €600 per month yields a gross return of 6%. After expenses and taxes, the realistic net yield settles closer to 4.2%.

City-by-City Breakdown of Bulgarian Urban Markets

Each major Bulgarian city operates under distinct economic drivers, influencing both property acquisition costs and monthly rental rates.

Sofia: The Corporate and Tech Capital

Sofia commands the highest property prices in the country, with prime districts like Lozenets, Iztok, and the Center often exceeding €2,000 to €2,500 per square meter. Yet, strong demand from multinational corporations, tech hubs, and educational institutions keeps long-term vacancy rates remarkably low. While gross yields in central Sofia can be compressed (around 4% to 5%) due to inflated property values, rapidly developing neighborhoods near metro stations (such as Mladost or Krasno Selo) frequently deliver healthier yields closer to 6%.

Plovdiv: The Cultural and Industrial Hub

As Bulgaria’s second-largest city, Plovdiv boasts a thriving manufacturing sector, a booming outsourcing industry, and a vibrant student population. Property prices are significantly more accessible than in Sofia, while rental demand remains robust. Investors targeting areas near the International Fair or the city center often achieve gross yields ranging from 6% to 7.5%, making Plovdiv a favored destination for balanced capital growth and steady cash flow.

Varna and Burgas: The Coastal Metropolises

Unlike seasonal resort hotspots, Varna and Burgas function as active year-round economic engines featuring universities, major ports, and expanding commercial industries. Rental yields here benefit from a dual-market dynamic: steady long-term tenants from September to June, followed by potential short-term holiday monetization during the peak summer months. However, for pure long-term predictability, standard multi-year lease agreements in districts like Varna’s Vladislavovo or Burgas’s Lazur offer stable gross returns averaging 5.5% to 6.8%.

Realistic Calculation of Acquisition and Operational Costs

Calculating your net yield accurately requires accounting for every lev and euro spent before and after closing the deal. Failing to budget for these items can dramatically skew your return on investment.

Upfront Acquisition Costs (Approx. 4% – 7%)

  • Notary and Municipal Fees: Roughly 1.5% to 3% of the property’s tax valuation or purchase price (whichever is higher).
  • Local Agency Commission: Typically 3% plus VAT if you utilize a broker.
  • Legal Fees: Around €300 to €1,000 depending on the complexity of the title search and transaction.

Ongoing Operational Expenses

  • Property Management: If you reside abroad or prefer a hands-off approach, local property management companies charge between 8% and 12% of the monthly gross rent.
  • Maintenance and Repairs: Budget at least 5% to 10% of annual rental income for wear-and-tear upkeep, appliance replacements, and building facade fees.
  • Building Fund (Etazhna Sobstvenost): Monthly maintenance fees for common areas, elevators, and security, usually ranging from €10 to €40 per month.

Note: If you are purchasing a property still under construction, ensure you thoroughly understand the legal implications of Bulgarian property acts (Act 14, 15, and 16), as unexpected construction delays can severely disrupt your projected timeline for generating rental income.

Navigating Bulgarian Property Taxes and Legal Obligations

Tax compliance is critical for foreign and domestic landlords operating within Bulgaria. Fortunately, the country maintains one of the most competitive tax regimes in the European Union, though absolute adherence to local legislation is mandatory.

Income Tax on Rental Revenue

Rental income earned by physical persons (individuals) is subject to a flat 10% personal income tax. However, the calculation method is remarkably advantageous: Bulgarian tax law allows for a legally mandated 10% normative deduction for expenses (representing maintenance and upkeep), meaning you are only taxed on 90% of your gross rental income.

Example Calculation:

  • Gross Annual Rent: €7,200 (€600/month)
  • Normative Expense Deduction (10%): €720
  • Taxable Base: €6,480
  • Income Tax Due (10% of Taxable Base): €648

Landlords must declare their rental income annually via the personal tax return (submitted by April 30 of the following year) and make quarterly advance payments if renting to legal entities (companies), though individual-to-individual leases generally operate on annual declarations.

Local Municipal Taxes

Beyond income tax, property owners must pay two recurring annual municipal levies to the local municipality:

  • Real Estate Tax: Typically ranges between 0.1% and 0.45% of the property's tax valuation (which is almost always significantly lower than market value).
  • Garbage Collection Fee: Varies heavily by municipality and zone, calculated based on the property's tax evaluation or intended commercial use.

Strategies to Optimize Your Long-Term Rental Yield

To maximize profitability in competitive urban environments, strategic property selection and presentation are paramount.

Strategy Impact on Yield Implementation Tip
Proximity to Infrastructure High (+1% to +2%) Target apartments within a 10-minute walking radius of metro stations (Sofia) or major university campuses.
Targeted Modernization Medium (+0.5% to +1.5%) Invest in energy-efficient appliances, durable flooring, and contemporary minimalist furniture to attract high-paying corporate tenants.
Energy Efficiency (EPC) Medium (Utility Savings) Properties with high energy efficiency ratings command lower heating/cooling bills, reducing tenant turnover.

Conclusion

Investing in long-term rentals across Bulgaria’s major cities offers a stable, predictable avenue for wealth generation, provided you approach the market with realistic financial modeling. While gross yields can look enticing on paper, factoring in acquisition overheads, maintenance contingencies, and the flat 10% income tax ensures your investment strategy remains resilient. By focusing on high-demand micro-locations, maintaining transparency with tax authorities, and meeting modern tenant expectations, urban Bulgarian real estate can serve as a profitable cornerstone of a diversified international portfolio.
