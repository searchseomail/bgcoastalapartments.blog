Bulgarian Property Acts Explained: Act 14, 15, and 16

MysticMaven
30.07.2026

Navigating the Bulgarian real estate market can be an incredibly rewarding venture, whether you are looking for a sunny Black Sea holiday home, a Sofia city apartment, or a tranquil rural retreat. However, buying property in Eastern Europe comes with its own unique legal framework that every international investor must understand. If you have been browsing Bulgarian property listings, you have undoubtedly encountered three critical milestones: Act 14, Act 15, and Act 16. These are not merely bureaucratic checkboxes; they are official legal stages of construction that dictate the structural completeness, legal status, and habitability of a building. For a comprehensive overview of the entire acquisition process, you can refer to our Step-by-Step Guide to Buying Property in Bulgaria as a Foreigner, which breaks down everything from initial search to final notary deeds.

Understanding these acts can mean the difference between a lucrative investment and a legal nightmare. In this detailed guide, we will break down exactly what Act 14, Act 15, and Act 16 mean, how they affect your purchasing rights, and what risks you need to look out for at every stage of development.

What Are the Bulgarian Construction Acts?

In Bulgaria, the construction and commissioning of residential and commercial buildings are strictly regulated by the Spatial Development Act (Zakon za ustroystvo na teritoriyata - ZUT). To ensure safety, compliance with architectural plans, and adherence to urban zoning laws, the construction process is broken down into specific phases. Each phase culminates in a legally binding protocol or "Act" signed by the construction company, the supervisor, the designer, and local municipal authorities.

For buyers, these acts represent risk reduction. Buying off-plan (before construction starts) offers the lowest entry price, but the highest risk. Buying at Act 16 offers ultimate security, but at market value. Let us examine each milestone in detail.

Act 14: The "Rough Construction" Phase

What is Act 14?

Act 14 (Akt 14) certifies that the building has achieved "rough construction" (in Bulgarian: akt na obzavezhdane or gruob stroezh). Officially, this means the building has a completed structural skeleton. Concrete framework, exterior walls, interior partitions, and the roof are fully in place.

Key Characteristics of Act 14:

  • The building can now be recognized as a distinct physical entity (immovable property).
  • Individual apartments acquire distinct physical boundaries, clear square footage, and individual architectural numbers.
  • Buyers can officially sign a Preliminary Contract or even a Final Notary Deed (though the latter carries significant risk if construction stalls afterwards).

Legal and Financial Implications

Reaching Act 14 is a major psychological and financial milestone for both developer and buyer. Legally, once Act 14 is signed, the risk of accidental destruction of the property (e.g., acts of God) typically transfers from the builder to the buyer, depending on your specific contract terms. From an investment perspective, buying at Act 14 usually offers a discount compared to finished properties, but you must thoroughly vet the developer's financial stability to ensure they can cross the finish line.

Act 15: Completion of Construction Works

What is Act 15?

Act 15 (Akt 15) is the document that states the construction works have been fully completed according to the approved architectural designs and technical standards. At this stage, the building is physically finished, but it has not yet been officially approved for human habitation by the state authorities.

What is Included in Act 15?

  • All interior and exterior finishing works (plastering, painting of common areas, installation of windows, doors, and balconies).
  • Installation of basic infrastructure (elevators, wiring, plumbing).
  • Establishment of a handover protocol between the investor and the construction contractor, listing any minor defects or cosmetic flaws that must be rectified.

Buyer's Perspective at Act 15

At Act 15, you can physically walk through your apartment, inspect the walls, measure the rooms, and point out any discrepancies. However, you still cannot legally move in or hook up permanent household utility contracts (electricity and water) at residential tariffs. Purchasing a property at this stage means you are very close to the finish line, and prices will reflect that increased security.

Act 16: Permission for Use (Habitation Permit)

What is Act 16?

Act 16 is the holy grail of Bulgarian real estate. Officially known as the Certificate for Permission to Use (Razreshenie za polzvane), it is issued by the Directorate for National Construction Control (DNNS) or the local municipality. This document certifies that the building is completely safe, fully compliant with all fire safety, environmental, and structural regulations, and ready for people to live in.

Why Act 16 is Crucial:

  • Legal Habitation: It is officially illegal to live in a residential building in Bulgaria that lacks Act 16.
  • Utility Contracts: Only with Act 16 can you sign direct, permanent contracts with local utility providers (electricity, water, central heating) at domestic rates. Without it, builders often supply "industrial" electricity, which is significantly more expensive.
  • Mortgage Eligibility: Bulgarian banks will rarely approve a mortgage for a property that does not have Act 16.
  • Resale Value: Properties with Act 16 command significantly higher resale and rental yields because they eliminate buyer uncertainty.

Comparison Table: Act 14 vs. Act 15 vs. Act 16

Feature Act 14 (Rough Construction) Act 15 (Completed Construction) Act 16 (Permission for Use)

Can You Live There? No. No (illegal and unsafe). Yes, completely legal.

Common Pitfalls and Red Flags to Watch Out For

Foreign investors often fall into traps set by unscrupulous developers or misleading marketing campaigns. Keep these crucial points in mind:

  • "Act 16 is coming next month": If a developer tells you that Act 16 is imminent while the building is still at Act 14, be very cautious. Administrative hurdles, bureaucratic backlogs, and utility connection delays can stall Act 16 for months—or even years.
  • Living on Industrial Tariffs: Some developments stall indefinitely between Act 15 and Act 16 due to minor regulatory infractions by the builder. Buyers move in anyway, only to find themselves paying exorbitant commercial electricity rates and struggling with water pressure issues.
  • The "Certificate for Commissioning" Misconception: Ensure your lawyer thoroughly checks that the Act 16 document is fully registered and unencumbered by municipal disputes or unpaid builder fines.

Conclusion

Navigating the nuances of Bulgarian property acts—Act 14, Act 15, and Act 16—is an essential skill for anyone looking to invest in Bulgaria's dynamic real estate market. Whether you choose to buy early off-plan to maximize capital appreciation or wait until Act 16 for peace of mind and immediate rental readiness, always work with an independent, English-speaking real estate lawyer. By verifying these milestones personally through official documentation rather than taking a developer's word for it, you will safeguard your investment and ensure a smooth, stress-free property acquisition experience.
