Can Foreigners Buy Land in Bulgaria? Why You Need to Register a Company (OOD)

MysticMaven
02.08.2026 • Read time: 8 min

Bulgaria’s stunning Black Sea coastline, majestic mountains, and remarkably low cost of living have made it one of Europe's best-kept secrets for real estate investors and lifestyle buyers alike. Whether you are dreaming of a rustic countryside villa, a modern ski chalet, or a coastal apartment, the market offers incredible value. However, foreign nationals frequently encounter a distinct legal hurdle when navigating the Bulgarian property market: the constitutional restriction on foreign ownership of land.

While buying an apartment or a built-up property without land is relatively straightforward, purchasing a house with a plot of land or a vacant plot of earth is subject to strict regulations based on your nationality and residency status. To legally bypass these restrictions and secure your dream plot, setting up a Bulgarian company—specifically a Limited Liability Company, locally known as an OOD (Ogranicheno Otgovornost Druzhestvo)—is the most efficient, legally sound, and widely used strategy. This comprehensive guide explores the nuances of property acquisition, why establishing an OOD is essential, and how to navigate the process seamlessly.

Can Foreigners Buy Land in Bulgaria? The Legal Framework

To understand why you might need a company, you must first understand Bulgaria’s dual approach to real estate ownership. Under Article 22 of the Constitution of the Republic of Bulgaria and the Ownership Act, the rules are divided into two main categories:

  • Buildings and Apartments: Foreign nationals from both inside and outside the European Union (EU) and the European Economic Area (EEA) have the absolute right to buy buildings, apartments, and commercial spaces without any legal restrictions or the need for a local company.
  • Land (Undeveloped Plots and Land Attached to Houses): Non-EU/EEA citizens do not have the right to acquire ownership of land in Bulgaria directly in their own name as physical persons.

For EU and EEA citizens, the landscape is slightly different. Following Bulgaria’s accession to the EU, transitional periods that banned EU citizens from buying land expired. Today, EU citizens can buy land directly in their own name, provided they use it for permanent residence or establish residency in Bulgaria. However, citizens from countries outside the EU (such as the UK, Russia, the US, or Canada) are strictly prohibited from purchasing land as private individuals. For these buyers, utilizing a corporate structure is not just an option—it is a necessity.

Exceptions to the Rule

There are very few exceptions to the land ownership ban for non-EU individuals. Foreigners who inherit Bulgarian property by law can acquire land, but this is contingent on specific familial ties. Additionally, foreign citizens with permanent residency in Bulgaria may sometimes navigate certain property types, but these exemptions are narrow. For the vast majority of non-EU investors, forming an OOD remains the gold standard for full legal compliance.

What is a Bulgarian OOD?

An OOD (Обявено Дружество с Ограничена Отговорност) is the Bulgarian equivalent of a Limited Liability Company (LLC). It is the most popular corporate vehicle for foreign investors, small business owners, and real estate buyers because it offers personal asset protection and operational flexibility.

Key characteristics of a Bulgarian OOD include:

  • Limited Liability: Shareholders are only liable for the company's debts up to the amount of their contributed capital, protecting your personal wealth.
  • Low Capital Requirement: The minimum required registered capital to set up an OOD is a symbolic 2 Bulgarian Lev (BGN), which is roughly equivalent to 1 Euro.
  • Single Ownership Option: If you are buying the property alone, you can register an EOOD (ЕООД), which is a Sole Proprietor Limited Liability Company with just one director and owner.
  • Foreign Ownership: A foreign national—regardless of residency status—can own 100% of the shares in a Bulgarian OOD and act as its manager.

Why You Need an OOD to Buy Land with a House

If you find a charming Bulgarian country home or a villa that sits on a plot of land, you are technically buying two things: the building and the land beneath and around it. Since non-EU citizens cannot own the land directly, the transaction cannot be completed in your personal name.

By registering an OOD, the company acts as a local Bulgarian legal entity. Because the company is registered in Bulgaria, it holds full domestic legal status, meaning it possesses the exact same rights as a Bulgarian citizen when it comes to acquiring real estate. The OOD purchases the property, meaning the company holds the title deeds (Notary Acts) for both the building and the land.

To successfully execute this, you should follow a Step-by-Step Guide to Buying Property in Bulgaria as a Foreigner to ensure that every stage—from finding the right notary to drafting the preliminary contract—is handled correctly under corporate ownership.

The Process of Setting Up a Bulgarian OOD

Establishing an OOD is a relatively fast and straightforward process, typically taking between 5 to 10 working days once all documentation is prepared. Here is what the process generally entails:

1. Choosing a Company Name

You must check the availability of your desired company name in the Bulgarian Commercial Register and reserve it if it is unique.

2. Drafting Articles of Association

The constitutional documents of the company must be drafted. These outline the company’s name, registered address, scope of business activities, management structure, and the distribution of shares.

3. Opening a Temporary Capital Bank Account

You will need to open a temporary escrow or capital account in a Bulgarian bank and deposit the minimum share capital (at least 2 BGN).

4. Submission to the Commercial Register

The application, along with specimen signatures (specimen of the manager's signature legalized before a notary), ID copies, and incorporation fees, is submitted to the Registry Agency. Once approved, your company receives a unique UIC (Unified Identification Code) and officially exists.

While it is possible to handle this remotely via a Power of Attorney (POA) given to a local Bulgarian lawyer, many investors prefer to visit briefly to open the bank account and meet with professionals.

Hidden Costs and Ongoing Responsibilities of Owning a Property via an OOD

While setting up a company solves the land-ownership dilemma, it is important to remember that a registered OOD is an active legal entity. This comes with ongoing administrative and financial obligations that you must budget for.

First, you need to be aware of the Hidden Costs and Taxes When Buying Real Estate in Bulgaria: What to Budget For. Beyond the initial purchase price, transaction taxes, notary fees, and local council taxes apply whether you buy as an individual or a company.

Second, maintaining a company incurs yearly expenses. Even if your OOD is set up solely to hold property and generates no commercial income, it is legally required to:

  • Hire a certified Bulgarian accountant to file annual financial statements and tax returns.
  • Pay corporate tax on any profits (flat rate of 10% in Bulgaria), though holding a non-commercial property usually results in zero taxable business profit.
  • File annual zero-activity reports with the National Revenue Agency and the National Statistical Institute if the company is dormant.

Accounting fees for a dormant property-holding OOD are generally quite low, but they are a mandatory recurring expense that you must factor into your long-term budget.

Important Technical Considerations: Act Status

Whether you are purchasing land to build your own home or buying a completed house through your OOD, pay close attention to the construction milestones. In Bulgaria, buildings are categorized by legal construction stages known as Acts. Before finalizing any purchase agreement, ensure you understand the differences outlined in Bulgarian Property Acts Explained: Act 14, 15, and 16. Buying a property that lacks Act 16 (the final certificate of occupancy) can create severe complications regarding utility hookups, legal habitation rights, and company asset valuation.

Conclusion

Can foreigners buy land in Bulgaria? Yes, but if you are a non-EU citizen, you cannot do so directly as an individual. Registering a Bulgarian OOD is the legal, secure, and widely accepted pathway to overcoming this restriction. It grants you full ownership of houses with land, undeveloped plots, and commercial real estate while offering liability protection.

Although owning property through a company introduces minor administrative responsibilities, such as annual accounting and reporting, the benefits far outweigh the hurdles. By assembling a reliable team of local real estate agents, legal counsel, and accountants, you can smoothly navigate the Bulgarian market and secure your dream Mediterranean or mountain retreat with complete peace of mind.
