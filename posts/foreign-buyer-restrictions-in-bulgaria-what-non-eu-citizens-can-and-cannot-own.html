Foreign Buyer Restrictions in Bulgaria: What Non-EU Citizens Can and Cannot Own

ByteSized
08.09.2026 • Read time: 6 min

Bulgaria has long captured the imagination of international property hunters thanks to its dramatic mountain ranges, affordability, and stunning Black Sea coastline. Whether you are dreaming of a ski chalet, a sunny retirement retreat, or a cozy countryside escape, the Bulgarian real estate market offers incredible value. However, for non-European Union (non-EU) citizens—often referred to as third-country nationals—navigating the legal landscape requires a clear understanding of specific ownership rules. While EU citizens enjoy virtually the same property rights as Bulgarian nationals, non-EU buyers face a distinct set of legal hurdles, particularly regarding land ownership.

Understanding these regulations is the critical first step before you start browsing listings, making offers, or falling in love with a specific property. This comprehensive guide breaks down what non-EU citizens can and cannot legally own in Bulgaria, the legal workarounds available, and the practical steps to secure your dream property.

The Golden Rule of Bulgarian Property Law: Land vs. Buildings

To understand foreign buyer restrictions in Bulgaria, you must first grasp a fundamental distinction in Bulgarian civil law: the separation of ownership between a building and the land it sits upon. This distinction dictates everything a non-EU buyer can and cannot purchase.

  • Buildings (Apartments, Houses, Commercial Spaces): Non-EU citizens have the full legal right to own buildings, apartments, and independent structures anywhere in Bulgaria in their own personal name.
  • Land (Undeveloped Plots, Agricultural Land, Forests): Under the Bulgarian Constitution and the Ownership Act, foreign individuals and companies from outside the EU are strictly prohibited from owning land.

This dual rule creates a unique situation for detached houses. While a non-EU citizen can own the physical structure of a house, they technically cannot own the underlying plot of land. To bypass this restriction legally, foreign buyers typically establish a Bulgarian limited liability company (LTD), as local legal entities—regardless of foreign ownership—possess the full right to own land.

What Non-EU Citizens Can Own Directly (Without a Company)

If you prefer to purchase property directly in your personal name without the administrative overhead of setting up a local company, your options are quite broad, provided the purchase does not include accompanying land ownership.

Apartments and Condominiums

Buying an apartment in a multi-story building or a gated complex is the easiest route for a non-EU buyer. Because an apartment block sits on a plot of land collectively owned by all apartment owners (or leased), individual apartment buyers hold title deeds to their specific flat along with an ideal fraction of the common parts of the building. Crucially, this does not constitute direct ownership of restricted land. Whether you are looking for a ski getaway by exploring an apartment in Bansko for year-round mountain living or a seaside flat along the coast, standard apartment purchases are straightforward for individual foreign buyers.

Houses with Right to Build (Superficies)

If you purchase a detached house, it is still legally possible to buy the structure itself as a non-EU citizen if the land belongs to someone else or is held under a legal construct known as the "Right to Build" (*Pravo na Stroesh*). However, this can complicate property management, financing, and future resale, making it a less common path compared to company formation.

What Non-EU Citizens Cannot Own Directly

The Bulgarian state fiercely protects its natural resources and agricultural sectors. Consequently, several categories of real estate are strictly off-limits to direct ownership by non-EU individuals:

  • Agricultural Land: Fields, meadows, vineyards, orchards, and any land designated for farming cannot be purchased by non-EU citizens.
  • Forests and Woodland: All state and private forestry land is restricted.
  • Protected Natural Areas: Land situated within national parks, nature reserves, or ecologically sensitive zones.
  • Border Zones: Certain strategic border regions have special security restrictions that limit property acquisition by non-nationals.

For buyers interested in purchasing a traditional village home, this restriction is particularly important. Traditional Bulgarian rural houses almost always come bundled with a yard (regulated plot of land, or *dvor*). If a non-EU buyer wants a house with a garden, they cannot complete the purchase in their personal name because the yard constitutes land.

The Legal Solution: Setting Up a Bulgarian Company

Because the restrictions on land ownership apply specifically to *foreign individuals*, the most common and legally sound workaround for non-EU buyers is to register a Bulgarian commercial company (usually an OOD or EOOD, the equivalent of a Limited Liability Company).

Once established, the Bulgarian company is considered a domestic legal entity. Therefore, the company has the full constitutional right to buy, own, and sell any type of real estate in Bulgaria—including agricultural land, forests, and large plots attached to rural houses.

Key Considerations for Company Ownership:

  • Low Capital Requirement: The minimum capital required to register a Bulgarian LLC is symbolic (often just 2 BGN, or approximately 1 EUR), making setup very affordable.
  • Maintenance Costs: Owning property through a company means you must maintain basic corporate accounting, file annual tax returns, and keep the entity active. Hiring a local accountant is essential.
  • Taxes: Corporate tax in Bulgaria is a flat 10%, which is among the lowest in the European Union, making this structure advantageous for those looking to rent out their properties or build a real estate portfolio.

Step-by-Step Buying Process for Non-EU Nationals

Whether you choose to buy an apartment in your own name or set up a company to acquire a house with a large plot of land, the transaction process in Bulgaria follows a well-defined legal framework:

  1. Property Search and Due Diligence: Work with a reputable local real estate agency and an independent property lawyer. Ensure the seller holds a clean title deed and that there are no mortgages, executive liens, or third-party claims on the property.
  2. Tax Number (BULSTAT): Before purchasing property in a personal name, non-EU citizens must obtain a foreign identification number from the Bulgarian tax authority (NAP).
  3. Preliminary Contract: Once due diligence is cleared, a preliminary contract is signed, typically accompanied by a 10% deposit. This binds both parties legally.
  4. Notary Transfer: In Bulgaria, property ownership is officially transferred via a Notary Public. The final Title Deed (*Notaturen Akt*) is signed in the presence of a certified notary and registered in the Property Register.

For those aiming to settle down permanently, whether you are comparing peaceful coastal spots like the quiet charms of Ravda and Sarafovo or exploring bustling urban hubs, understanding these administrative steps ensures a smooth transition.

Conclusion

Foreign buyer restrictions in Bulgaria should not discourage you from pursuing your property goals; rather, they highlight the importance of proper legal preparation. While non-EU citizens cannot directly own land—such as agricultural plots or large rural yards—in their personal names, the simple and widely accepted solution of forming a Bulgarian company opens up the entire real estate market. By partnering with qualified local legal counsel and understanding the nuances of Bulgarian property law, you can safely and successfully invest in your slice of this beautiful European country.
