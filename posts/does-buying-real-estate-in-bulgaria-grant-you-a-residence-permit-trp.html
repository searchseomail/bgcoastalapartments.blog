Does Buying Real Estate in Bulgaria Grant You a Residence Permit (TRP)?

PixelPioneer
05.09.2026 • Read time: 7 min

Bulgaria has long captured the imagination of international real estate investors, retirees, and remote workers. With its stunning Black Sea coastline, picturesque mountain ranges, affordable cost of living, and strategic position within the European Union, it is little wonder that foreign buyers frequently ask: Does buying real estate in Bulgaria grant you a residence permit (TRP)?

The short and most accurate answer is: No, simply purchasing a property in Bulgaria does not automatically grant you a Temporary Residence Permit (TRP). Unlike some Mediterranean nations that offer direct "Golden Visa" programs tied exclusively to real estate acquisitions, Bulgarian immigration law operates under different rules. However, owning property in Bulgaria can still serve as a foundational stepping stone for obtaining legal residency through alternative legal pathways.

In this comprehensive guide, we will break down what property ownership actually means for your residency status, explore the legal avenues available for obtaining a Bulgarian TRP, and look at the practical steps you need to follow.

The Common Misconception: Real Estate vs. Residency

Many foreign nationals fall victim to widespread marketing myths suggesting that buying a cheap apartment on the coast or buying an apartment in Sofia immediately secures a visa or a long-term residence card. In reality, the Bulgarian Foreigners in the Republic of Bulgaria Act does not list "owning a residential property" as a standalone qualifying ground for a TRP.

What property ownership *does* guarantee you, however, is the right to a multiple-entry D-visa and subsequent residency if you qualify under another category—most notably, the "Representative of a Foreign Commercial Company" route, or by qualifying as a financially independent retiree.

Furthermore, before diving into the market, investors must understand local construction milestones, ensuring their chosen asset complies with legal standards such as those detailed in our guide on Bulgarian Property Acts Explained: Act 14, 15, and 16.

Legal Pathways to a Bulgarian Temporary Residence Permit (TRP)

Since buying real estate alone does not yield a residence permit, how can property owners legally live in Bulgaria for more than 90 days? There are several established routes under Bulgarian migration law:

1. Representative of a Foreign Commercial Company (Trade Representative Office)

This is historically the most popular method used by non-EU property owners. To qualify:

  • You must register a trade representative office (TRO) in Bulgaria for a foreign company that you own or manage.
  • The parent company must be actively operating abroad.
  • The Bulgarian Chamber of Commerce and Industry (BCCI) must approve the representative.
  • You must employ at least 10 Bulgarian citizens (though exemptions and specific criteria apply depending on recent legal updates, maintaining a physical office and showing commercial activity is crucial).

Once your TRO status is approved, you can apply for a Long-Term D-Visa and subsequently a TRP.

2. Financially Independent Pensioner Status

If you are of retirement age (according to your home country's standards) and have sufficient financial means, Bulgaria welcomes you:

  • You must prove receipt of a regular pension (state or private).
  • You must secure a place to live in Bulgaria—which is where owning real estate becomes immensely helpful, as having a registered address via a title deed simplifies the accommodation proof requirement.
  • You must open a Bulgarian bank account and show adequate subsistence funds.

3. Commercial Activity (OOD / EOOD Company Owner)

Instead of a representative office, you can set up a local Bulgarian company (analogous to an LLC). To secure a TRP through this route:

  • Your company must create at least 10 full-time jobs for Bulgarian citizens.
  • The company must actively conduct business and invest capital locally.

Step-by-Step Guide: Buying Property and Securing Residency

If your ultimate goal is to live in Bulgaria part-time or full-time using real estate as your anchor, you should follow a structured roadmap:

Step 1: Purchase the Real Estate

Foreigners from outside the EU can freely buy apartments and buildings in Bulgaria, though non-EU citizens cannot own the underlying land directly without setting up a local company. Ensure your transaction is legally sound, check all title documents, and secure your notary act.

Step 2: Establish Your Qualifying Grounds

Simultaneously or immediately after purchasing your property (whether it's a cozy urban flat or a property targeted for Golden Sands real estate investment), set up your chosen residency vehicle—such as registering a Trade Representative Office or preparing your pensioner status documentation.

Step 3: Apply for a D-Visa

You cannot apply for a TRP from inside Bulgaria if you are a non-EU citizen entering on a tourist waiver. You must apply for a Long-Term Stay Visa (Visa D) at the Bulgarian Embassy or Consulate in your home country. You will need to present:

  • Proof of accommodation (your property title deed or a long-term lease agreement).
  • Proof of financial subsistence (bank statements).
  • Clean criminal record certificate.
  • Comprehensive health insurance valid in Bulgaria.
  • Documents proving your qualifying status (e.g., BCCI certificate for a trade representative).

Step 4: Enter Bulgaria and Apply for the TRP

Once your D-Visa is granted (which typically takes 35 to 60 days), you can travel to Bulgaria, move into your property, and submit your application for a Temporary Residence Permit at the Migration Directorate of the Ministry of Interior.

Rights and Limitations of a Bulgarian TRP Holder

Holding a Temporary Residence Permit in Bulgaria brings substantial benefits:

  • Visa-free travel within the Schengen Area (since Bulgaria’s partial accession to Schengen for air and sea borders).
  • The ability to stay in Bulgaria continuously for longer than 90 days in every 180-day period.
  • Access to local healthcare systems upon contributing to the National Health Insurance Fund.
  • The right to bring dependent family members through family reunification procedures.

However, it is important to remember that a TRP is temporary—usually issued for one year and subject to annual renewal. After legally residing in Bulgaria with a TRP for 5 consecutive years (with limited absences), you may apply for Permanent Residence (PRP), which brings you one step closer to potential EU citizenship.

Conclusion

While buying real estate in Bulgaria does not automatically hand you the keys to a residence permit on a silver platter, it remains a critical and highly advantageous asset. Owning a home fulfills the strict accommodation requirements demanded by Bulgarian migration authorities, making the bureaucratic process of securing residency via retirement or business representation significantly smoother. By understanding the legal distinctions and preparing the correct paperwork, you can successfully turn your Bulgarian property investment into a gateway for legal European residency.
