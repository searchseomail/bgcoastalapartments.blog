Hidden Costs and Taxes When Buying Real Estate in Bulgaria: What to Budget For

AstroCoder
01.08.2026 • Read time: 6 min

Bulgaria continues to captivate international real estate investors, lifestyle buyers, and retirees looking for affordable European Union property. With its stunning Black Sea coastline, majestic ski resorts, and historically low property prices, the appeal is undeniable. However, many foreign buyers make the critical mistake of budgeting only for the listed purchase price. Failing to account for the secondary financial obligations can derail your investment and create unexpected financial strain.

When purchasing real estate in this Balkan nation, various transaction taxes, state fees, legal costs, and ongoing expenses can add anywhere from 4% to 10% (and sometimes more) on top of the agreed-upon property price. Understanding these hidden costs from the outset is essential for a smooth transaction. This comprehensive guide breaks down every single expense you need to budget for when buying property in Bulgaria.

Initial Transaction Taxes and Government Fees

The moment you decide to purchase a property, state and local authorities levy several mandatory fees. These are usually calculated based on the higher of two values: the agreed purchase price in the preliminary contract or the official tax valuation (*danatsenna otsenka*) determined by the local municipality.

1. Local Municipal Tax (Property Transfer Tax)

This is typically the largest single tax you will pay during the acquisition process. It ranges between 0.1% and 3% of the property’s value, depending on the municipality where the real estate is located. For instance, popular areas like Varna, Burgas, and Sofia generally set this fee around 2.5% to 3%. By convention, this cost is usually split 50/50 between the buyer and the seller, but in a seller's market, buyers are often pressured to cover the entire amount.

2. Notary Fees

In Bulgaria, property ownership transfer must be formalized in front of a state or private notary. Notary fees are strictly regulated by law and scale progressively based on the property’s value. On average, expect to pay between 0.5% and 1.5% of the property value. If you cannot be present in Bulgaria to sign the deeds in person, you will also need to budget for a power of attorney, drafting fees, and certified translations.

3. Registry Agency (Cadastre and Property Register) Fees

Once the notary signs the title deed, it must be entered into the Bulgarian Property Register (*Imoten Registar*) managed by the Registry Agency to legally secure your ownership. This service incurs a state fee, usually around 0.1% of the property value. Additionally, issuing a certificate of tax valuation and obtaining sketches from the Cadastre Agency will cost a small administrative fee.

Professional Representation and Intermediary Costs

Navigating the Bulgarian real estate market independently can be perilous, especially for non-residents facing a language barrier and unfamiliar legal frameworks. Hiring professionals adds to the upfront budget, but it is an investment in security.

1. Real Estate Agency Commission

If you use a real estate broker to find your property, expect to pay a commission. The standard market rate in Bulgaria is generally 3% of the purchase price (plus VAT), though some agencies charge a flat fee for lower-priced properties (usually a minimum of €1,000 to €2,000). Always clarify upfront whether the commission is subject to VAT and whether it is payable by the buyer, the seller, or both.

2. Legal Fees

Relying solely on the real estate agent is rarely enough; independent legal counsel is strongly recommended. A qualified property lawyer will conduct comprehensive due diligence—such as reviewing the legal status of the property to uncover any hidden debts, mortgages, or third-party claims. Legal fees typically range from €500 to €1,500 depending on the complexity and value of the transaction.

3. Translation and Interpretation Costs

By Bulgarian law, if a buyer does not fluent in Bulgarian, a licensed sworn interpreter must be present at the notary signing. Furthermore, all transaction documents must be officially translated. Budget around €100 to €300 for translation and interpretation services.

Hidden Costs Specific to Off-Plan and Under-Construction Properties

Many buyers are attracted to Bulgaria's booming developments, particularly along the coast and in ski towns. Buying off-plan or during construction offers lower entry prices, but it comes with distinct structural and financial variables.

Before committing to an unfinished development, it is vital to understand the legal stages of construction known as Act 14, Act 15, and Act 16. Purchasing a property that has not yet reached Act 16 (the official habitation permit) means you may face additional utility connection fees once the building is completed. Developers often charge extra for hooking up individual water and electricity meters, a cost that can catch uninitiated buyers off-guard.

Post-Purchase and Ongoing Annual Expenses

Once the ink has dried and you hold the keys to your new Bulgarian home, a new set of recurring expenses begins. Failing to budget for these ongoing costs can lead to municipal penalties or interrupted utility services.

1. Local Property Tax and Garbage Collection Fee

Every property owner in Bulgaria must pay annual municipal taxes. These consist of:

  • Annual Property Tax: Generally ranges between 0.05% and 0.45% of the tax valuation of the property, varying by municipality.
  • Garbage Collection Fee: Calculated based on the location and the size/type of the property. This fee is mandatory even if the property is used only seasonally.

2. Maintenance and Management Fees (Complexes and Gated Communities)

If you purchase an apartment in a gated complex, a coastal resort, or a managed building with amenities like swimming pools, security, elevators, and landscaped gardens, you will be subject to an annual maintenance fee (often referred to as a "support tax"). These fees can range drastically—from €4 to €15 per square meter per year. In luxury developments, this can translate to thousands of euros annually, which must be factored into your long-term holding costs.

3. Utilities (Electricity, Water, Internet)

Utility bills in Bulgaria are generally lower than in Western Europe, but they fluctuate based on usage and location. Heating in winter (especially if relying on electricity or air conditioning) can spike bills significantly. Additionally, changing the utility accounts (*partidi*) into your name after purchase involves a minor administrative fee for each utility provider (EVN, CEZ, Energo-Pro, and local water companies).

Final Financial Preparedness

Buying real estate in Bulgaria is a rewarding venture, provided you approach it with financial clarity. To ensure a stress-free process, it is recommended to add a buffer of at least 8% to 10% of the property's purchase price to cover all taxes, notary expenses, legal guidance, and initial administrative setup.
