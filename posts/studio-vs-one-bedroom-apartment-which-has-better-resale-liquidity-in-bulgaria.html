Studio vs One-Bedroom Apartment: Which Has Better Resale Liquidity in Bulgaria?

QuantumQuester
29.09.2026 • Read time: 7 min

Navigating the Bulgarian real estate market requires a keen understanding of buyer psychology, regional economic drivers, and long-term liquidity trends. Whether you are eyeing a Black Sea holiday home, a rental property in Sofia, or a mountain retreat in Bansko, one of the most common dilemmas investors face is choosing between a studio and a one-bedroom apartment. While both asset classes have their merits, their resale liquidity—how quickly and easily an asset can be converted into cash without drastically dropping its price—varies significantly depending on the location, target demographic, and prevailing market conditions.

In this comprehensive guide, we will analyze the resale liquidity of studios versus one-bedroom apartments in Bulgaria, examining factors such as capital appreciation, target buyer pools, maintenance costs, and market resilience.

Understanding Resale Liquidity in the Bulgarian Context

Liquidity is the lifeblood of real estate investment. A high-liquidity property can be sold within weeks of listing, whereas a low-liquidity property might sit on portals like Imot.bg for months or even years. Bulgaria’s property market is bifurcated: urban centers (Sofia, Plovdiv, Varna) are driven by local professionals, students, and nuclear families, while coastal and resort regions (such as Sunny Beach, Sveti Vlas, and Real estate in Sozopol: Old Town charm, prices, and rental potential) are heavily influenced by foreign buyers, digital nomads, and domestic holidaymakers.

When evaluating liquidity, we must look at the depth of the buyer pool. The wider the demographic appeal, the higher the liquidity. Let’s break down how studios and one-bedroom units measure up against this crucial metric.

Studio Apartments: Pros, Cons, and Liquidity Dynamics

Studio apartments—often referred to as efficiency apartments—consist of a single open-plan room combining the living area, bedroom, and kitchen, usually paired with a separate bathroom. Their primary selling point is affordability.

Advantages of Studios for Resale

  • Lower Entry Price: Because of their smaller square footage, studios require a lower absolute investment capital. This makes them accessible to a vast pool of first-time buyers and budget-conscious investors.
  • High Rental Yield Potential: In prime urban locations and tourist hotspots, studios often yield a higher return on investment (ROI) relative to their purchase price because rental demand for cheap accommodation is perpetually high.
  • Lower Maintenance Costs: Smaller spaces mean lower utility bills, cheaper furnishing costs, and reduced renovation expenses when preparing the property for resale.

Drawbacks Impacting Liquidity

  • Niche Buyer Pool: Studios are rarely suitable for long-term living by couples, families, or pet owners. Consequently, when you decide to sell, your target market is largely restricted to single professionals, students, or short-term rental operators.
  • Resale Price Ceilings: In certain resort complexes, oversabunance of studios can lead to fierce competition during a market downturn, forcing sellers to slash prices.

One-Bedroom Apartments: The Versatile Market Favorite

A standard one-bedroom apartment features a separate bedroom, a living room with a kitchen area (or separate kitchen), and a bathroom. This configuration represents the sweet spot of modern housing design for single occupants and young couples.

Why One-Bedroom Units Usually Win on Liquidity

  • Broad Target Audience: A one-bedroom layout appeals to singles who want extra space, young couples planning for the future, landlords targeting corporate tenants, and retirees looking to downsize. This broad demographic ensures a steady stream of prospective buyers.
  • Adaptability to Market Shifts: If the short-term holiday rental market dips, a one-bedroom apartment can easily be transitioned into a long-term residential rental, protecting the investor from prolonged vacancy.
  • Better Long-Term Value Retention: Land and build quality aside, properties with functional division tend to hold their value better over decades because consumer preferences rarely shift away from having a separate bedroom.

Location Matters: Urban vs. Resort Liquidity

The liquidity battle between studios and one-bedroom units changes drastically depending on where the property is located in Bulgaria.

1. Major Cities (Sofia, Plovdiv, Varna, Burgas)

In urban centers, one-bedroom apartments are the undisputed kings of liquidity. Young Bulgarian professionals prefer buying 1-BR units as their first independent homes. While low-priced studios near universities (like Sofia University or the Technical University of Varna) sell quickly due to student demand, families and career-oriented buyers bypass them entirely. If you are investing in a city, a one-bedroom unit offers a safer and faster exit strategy.

2. Coastal and Mountain Resorts

The resort property market operates under entirely different rules. In holiday hotspots, buyers are predominantly looking for low-maintenance vacation pads or investment vehicles for platforms like Airbnb and Booking.com. Here, studios punch above their weight. Because holidaymakers spend very little time indoors, a well-located studio near the beach or ski lift is extremely easy to flip. Furthermore, if you are analyzing high-end segments, such as properties outlined in First-line sea view apartments in Bulgaria: Is the extra cost really worth it?, buyers are often willing to pay a premium for views, regardless of whether it is a studio or a 1-BR unit.

Hidden Liquidity Killers to Watch Out For

Regardless of whether you choose a studio or a one-bedroom layout, several factors can severely harm your property’s resale liquidity in Bulgaria:

  • Excessive Maintenance Fees (Taksa Poddrazhka): Gated resort complexes with high annual maintenance fees (€10–€15+ per square meter) are notoriously difficult to resell because savvy buyers calculate these ongoing costs into their purchase decision.
  • Poor Execution and Layout: Oddly shaped rooms, internal bathrooms without ventilation, or walk-through kitchens will repel buyers even if the price is right.
  • Lack of Act 16: Properties sold "on green" or without official Act 16 documentation carry high legal risks, drastically shrinking your buyer pool to only cash-rich, high-risk speculators.

Conclusion: Which Option Guarantees Better Liquidity?

Ultimately, one-bedroom apartments generally offer superior and more stable resale liquidity across the entirety of the Bulgarian real estate market. They strike the optimal balance between affordability and functional living space, ensuring that your pool of potential buyers remains as wide as possible.

However, studios remain a viable, high-liquidity alternative only if purchased in hyper-strategic locations—such as downtown Sofia for university rentals or right by the beachfront in high-demand summer resorts. Before making a financial commitment, always analyze local micro-trends, assess the target demographic, and factor in future exit strategies to ensure your Bulgarian property investment remains agile and liquid.
