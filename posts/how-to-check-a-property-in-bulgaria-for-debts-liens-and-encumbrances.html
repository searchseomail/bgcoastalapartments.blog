How to Check a Property in Bulgaria for Debts, Liens, and Encumbrances

RetroRider
31.07.2026 • Read time: 5 min

Purchasing real estate in Bulgaria is an exciting venture, whether you are looking for a sunny coastal retreat in Sunny Beach, a mountain cabin in Bansko, or an apartment in Sofia. However, the allure of affordable European real estate can quickly turn into a financial nightmare if you fail to conduct thorough due diligence. One of the most critical steps in the acquisition process is uncovering whether the target property comes with hidden financial baggage. Unchecked debts, legal liens, and encumbrances can transfer to the new owner or, at worst, invalidate the purchase entirely.

Navigating the Bulgarian property market requires patience, local knowledge, and a meticulous approach to legal checks. To ensure your investment is secure, you must understand how to thoroughly investigate a property's history and current legal status. For a broader overview of the purchasing journey, including initial considerations, you can refer to this Step-by-Step Guide to Buying Property in Bulgaria as a Foreigner, which outlines the essential milestones every international buyer must navigate.

Understanding Property Liabilities in Bulgaria

Before diving into the procedural steps of checking a property, it is vital to understand what kinds of liabilities can be attached to real estate in Bulgaria. Unlike some jurisdictions where personal debts follow the individual, certain property-related debts and encumbrances "run with the land." This means that as the new owner, you could potentially inherit the legal headache.

Common issues found during title searches include:

  • Mortgages and Bank Liens: Outstanding loans taken by the previous owner using the property as collateral.
  • Tax Liabilities: Unpaid local municipal taxes and garbage collection fees.
  • Utility Arrears: Outstanding bills for electricity, water, and central heating.
  • Judicial Attachments (Bozhivanja / Vazgir): Freezes placed on the property by courts or bailiffs due to lawsuits or unpaid debts.
  • Right of Use (Usufruct) and Leases: Third-party legal rights allowing someone else to live in or use the property, sometimes even after it is sold.
  • Development and Construction Status Issues: For off-plan or new-build properties, ensuring the building has proper legal standing is crucial, which is closely tied to understanding Bulgarian Property Acts Explained: Act 14, 15, and 16.

The Cornerstone: The Property Register (Imoten Registar)

The single most authoritative source for verifying property ownership and encumbrances in Bulgaria is the Property Register, maintained by the Registry Agency (Agentsia po Vpisvaniyata). Every official property transaction, mortgage, and legal claim in Bulgaria must be recorded here to be legally binding against third parties.

To check the register, you (or your legal representative) will need specific details about the property, such as the Identifier (EKKAT), property number, volume, and batch number. The output of this search is known as a Certificate of Encumbrances ( удостоверение за тежести ).

What is a Certificate of Encumbrances?

The Certificate of Encumbrances is an official document issued by the Registry Agency. It details the history of the property ownership for a specified period (usually going back 10 or more years) and explicitly lists:

  • All registered mortgages (active or historical).
  • Sales, donations, and exchanges involving the property.
  • Injunctions, seizures, and pending litigation notices (vazgir).
  • Building prohibitions or expropriation notices.

If the certificate comes back clean—showing "no encumbrances" (bez tezezh)—it provides a strong baseline of security. However, this is only part of the puzzle, as certain operational debts do not automatically appear on the central property registry.

Step-by-Step Guide to Checking Debts and Liens

Conducting a comprehensive audit requires checking multiple institutions beyond just the Registry Agency. Below is a structured approach to uncovering all potential liabilities.

Type of Check Issuing Authority / Source What It Reveals
Certificate of Encumbrances Registry Agency (Imoten Registar) Mortgages, court injunctions, historical ownership, and legal claims.
Tax Clearance Certificate Local Municipality (Obshtina) Unpaid local real estate taxes and garbage collection fees.
Utility Status Checks EVN, CEZ/Electrohold, Energo-Pro, Water & Sewage (V&K) Outstanding balances for electricity, water, and heating.
Notary Archive Check Licensed Bulgarian Notary Verification of the seller's original Title Deed (Notarialen Akt) and prior transactions.

1. Checking Local Municipality Taxes (Obshtina)

Every property owner in Bulgaria is legally obligated to pay annual real estate tax and garbage collection fees to the local municipality where the property is located. If the seller has neglected these payments, the debt is tied to the property. Although municipalities do not always place a formal lien in the Registry Agency for small tax debts, a notary will not execute a final purchase deed (Notarialen Akt) if there are outstanding municipal taxes. Always demand a tax clearance certificate from the seller before proceeding.

2. Verifying Utility Accounts

Utility debts (electricity, water, central heating) are a common trap for unwary buyers. In Bulgaria, utility accounts are typically held in the name of the individual consumer rather than the property itself, meaning utility companies may claim that debts belong to the previous owner. However, changing the account into your name can become a bureaucratic nightmare if the previous tenant left behind massive unpaid bills. Always require the seller to produce recent, zero-balance utility bills and accompany them to the utility providers to close or transfer accounts upon purchase.

3. Investigating Private and Corporate Liabilities of the Seller

If the seller is an individual or a company facing bankruptcy, execution proceedings, or divorce settlements, creditors might attempt to annul property sales made to defraud them (known under Bulgarian law as a *Paulian action* or *otmenitelno izpechane*). Your lawyer should check:

  • The Central Register of Special Pledges (Tzarestr) if buying from a company.
  • Private bailiff registries for active enforcement cases against the seller.
  • Bankruptcy registers to ensure the seller is solvent.

The Role of a Bulgarian Notary and Independent Lawyer

Many foreign buyers assume that the Bulgarian notary acts as their personal attorney. This is a dangerous misconception. In the Bulgarian legal system, the notary is an impartial state-delegated official whose primary job is to verify the identity of the parties, ensure the documents comply with statutory requirements, and officially transfer the title. While a notary *does* pull a basic Certificate of Encumbrances on the day of the signing, they do not conduct deep investigative work into past transactions, hidden builder disputes, or operational utility debts.

This is why hiring an independent, English-speaking Bulgarian lawyer who represents *only your interests* is non-negotiable. A qualified lawyer will:

  • Examine the chain of title (history of previous owners) to ensure no prior inheritance disputes or fraudulent sales invalidate your purchase.
  • Draft a robust Preliminary Contract (Predvaritelen dogovor) that includes penalty clauses if the seller fails to deliver the property free of encumbrances.
  • Perform final checks on the exact morning of the closing before any money changes hands.

Conclusion

Checking a property in Bulgaria for debts, liens, and encumbrances is not merely a formality—it is a rigorous due diligence process that safeguards your capital. By leveraging the official Property Register, obtaining tax clearance certificates, auditing utility accounts, and retaining an independent legal professional, you can drastically minimize your risks. Take your time, verify every document, and ensure that your Bulgarian property dream remains a secure and joyful reality.
