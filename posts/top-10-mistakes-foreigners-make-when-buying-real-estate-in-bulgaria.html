Top 10 Mistakes Foreigners Make When Buying Real Estate in Bulgaria

ShadowWalker
09.09.2026 • Read time: 6 min

Bulgaria has long captured the imagination of international real estate investors, retirees, and holiday-home seekers. With its charming Black Sea coastline, majestic ski resorts, affordable cost of living, and accession to the European Union, the country offers an attractive entry point into the European property market. However, purchasing real estate abroad always comes with unique legal, cultural, and financial hurdles. Too many foreign buyers dive into the Bulgarian property market unprepared, lured by seemingly rock-bottom prices, only to find themselves facing unexpected bureaucracy, hidden fees, or unsellable assets. To help you navigate this market safely, here are the top 10 mistakes foreigners make when buying real estate in Bulgaria and how you can avoid them.

1. Ignoring the Importance of Building Acts (Act 14, Act 15, and Act 16)

One of the most dangerous and common pitfalls for foreign buyers is misunderstanding the stages of construction in Bulgaria. The country has a strict legal framework governing how buildings are constructed and commissioned. Many buyers fall in love with off-plan developments without realizing the legal implications of the property's current status. To protect your investment, it is vital to thoroughly research Bulgarian property acts explained: Act 14, Act 15, and Act 16 before signing any binding agreements or handing over deposits.

Buying a property that lacks Act 16 (the official certificate of occupancy) can lead to nightmare scenarios, including inability to legally live in the building, lack of permanent utility hookups (paying commercial rates for water and electricity instead of residential), and severe delays if the developer goes bankrupt before finishing the project.

2. Underestimating Ongoing Maintenance and Management Fees

First-time buyers often calculate their budget solely based on the purchase price and standard closing costs, completely forgetting about the recurring expenses of property ownership. This is especially true for gated communities and modern complexes along the Black Sea coast. Before committing to a purchase, you should carefully analyze maintenance fees in Bulgarian seaside resorts, typical costs, and how to avoid overpaying.

Some developments charge exorbitant annual maintenance fees for amenities like swimming pools, 24/7 security, and landscaped gardens—fees that must be paid whether you use the property or not. Failing to pay these can lead to legal disputes with management companies and restricted access to the complex.

3. Failing to Conduct a Thorough Title and Encumbrance Check

Never assume that a property registry system in Bulgaria operates identically to that of your home country. A shocking number of foreign buyers purchase real estate without conducting an independent, comprehensive title search through a qualified local lawyer. Properties can carry hidden mortgages, outstanding debts from previous owners, pending court litigations, or third-party rights (such as a right of use or "usufruct"). Always ensure your independent legal counsel checks the Property Register at the Registry Agency to guarantee clean title deeds.

4. Skipping Independent Legal Representation

Relying solely on the real estate agent—especially one recommended or provided by the seller or developer—is a recipe for disaster. Real estate agents are incentivized to close the deal. Foreign buyers often try to save money by skipping independent legal counsel, viewing it as an unnecessary expense. In reality, an independent Bulgarian lawyer who specializes in real estate law will safeguard your interests, draft secure preliminary contracts, verify permits, and represent you during the final notary transfer.

5. Overestimating Rental Yields and Resale Potential

Many investors purchase Bulgarian property with the dream of generating passive income through holiday rentals. While areas like Sunny Beach and Nessebar are bustling in July and August, the tourism season is relatively short. Buyers frequently overestimate yearly occupancy rates and net rental income. If you are looking into Golden Sands real estate investment and whether you can make a profit on summer holiday rentals, you must run realistic financial models that factor in vacancy periods, platform commissions, cleaning services, and wear-and-tear.

6. Misunderstanding Land Ownership Laws for Non-EU Citizens

While EU citizens enjoy the same rights as Bulgarian citizens when it comes to purchasing buildings and land in Bulgaria, the rules are different for non-EU citizens (third-country nationals). Non-EU nationals can freely buy apartments and houses with built-up areas, but they cannot own land in their own name under Bulgarian constitutional law. To bypass this, non-EU buyers typically need to set up a Bulgarian limited liability company (EOOD or OOD) to purchase the land portion of a property. Failing to understand this distinction can derail your purchase plans entirely.

7. Neglecting Local Taxes, Notary Fees, and Closing Costs

The purchase price listed on the property advertisement is never the final amount you will pay. Buyers must budget an additional 4% to 6% of the purchase price to cover closing costs. These typically include:

  • Local Municipality Tax: Usually between 0.5% and 3% depending on the municipality.
  • Notary Fees: Scaled according to the property value.
  • Agency Commission: Standardly around 3% plus VAT.
  • Legal Fees: Usually 1% for lawyer representation.
  • Property Registration Fees: Nominal fees for entering the deed into the registry.

8. Falling for "Notary Value" Under-Declaration Traps

Historically, a common practice in Bulgaria was to declare a much lower property value on the official notary deed than the actual purchase price paid, in order to minimize local taxes and notary fees. Sellers often pressure foreign buyers into this arrangement. Do not agree to this. Doing so is illegal tax evasion. Furthermore, if you decide to sell the property later, your capital gains tax will be calculated based on the difference between the low purchase value and your selling price, resulting in a massive tax penalty. It also leaves you with zero legal recourse to recover your actual invested money if the contract is declared void.

9. Ignoring the Practicalities of Property Management from Afar

Buying a holiday home and leaving it unattended for ten months of the year can lead to severe headaches. Freezing pipes in winter, moisture and mold buildup in coastal areas, unauthorized squatters, or storm damage require localized attention. Many foreigners make the mistake of not setting up a reliable local property management contract. Having a trusted local partner who can conduct regular property inspections, pay utility bills on your time, and handle emergency repairs is essential for peace of mind.

10. Skipping Due Diligence on Utilities and Infrastructure

Do not assume that every property has stable, individual connections to water, electricity, and sewage networks. Some rural houses or poorly planned suburban developments rely on shared transformers, private water wells, or septic tanks that may not meet modern regulatory or sanitary standards. Similarly, check the quality of internet connectivity if you plan on working remotely. Always request recent utility bills from the seller to confirm that there are no outstanding debts and that the supplies are connected to official municipal grids rather than temporary construction lines.

Summary Checklist for Foreign Buyers in Bulgaria

Mistake Consequence How to Avoid
Buying without Act 16 Higher utility tariffs, illegal habitation risks Verify construction paperwork through a lawyer
Skipping independent lawyers Undiscovered debts, fraudulent title deeds Hire an independent real estate attorney
Under-declaring purchase price Tax fraud, high capital gains later, no legal safety Insist on writing the real purchase price on the deed
Ignoring maintenance fees Unbudgeted expenses, legal disputes Calculate recurring fees before making an offer

Purchasing real estate in Bulgaria can be a deeply rewarding venture—whether you are securing a sun-drenched coastal retreat, a cozy ski chalet, or a high-yielding rental asset. By avoiding these ten common mistakes, practicing thorough due diligence, and working with trusted, independent professionals, you can protect your capital and enjoy your Bulgarian property investment for years to come.
